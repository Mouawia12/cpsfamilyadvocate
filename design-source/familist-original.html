Understanding Your Rights

A CPS investigation can feel overwhelming. Understanding your rights and the process helps you protect your family and work toward the best outcome.

Get Support Now

Your Rights During Investigation

  • Right to know allegations — CPS must inform you
  • Right to an attorney — Consult before speaking
  • Right to refuse entry — Without court order
  • Right to remain silent — Limited answers allowed
  • Right to support person — Bring someone
  • Right to record — Many states allow it
  • Right to appeal — Challenge determinations

Supporting a Family Member

When someone you care about is involved with CPS, knowing how to help—and what not to do—matters.

  • Offer to attend meetings as support person
  • Help with transportation to services
  • Assist with documentation and organization
  • Provide childcare during appointments
  • Offer emotional support without judgment
  • Help research resources and programs

What to Avoid

  • Speaking to caseworkers without permission
  • Criticizing CPS workers publicly
  • Enabling behaviors that led to involvement
  • Interfering with case plan requirements
  • Making promises you cannot keep
  • Sharing case details on social media

About Us

The Familist CPS Advocacy Model

This service was created by a dual board-certified mental health clinician with extensive CPS system experience. Our familist approach recognizes that child safety, parental capacity, and family stability are interconnected—allowing families to receive guidance that aligns with how CPS actually evaluates risk, progress, and parental capacity while strengthening the entire family system.

A familist approach recognizes that child safety, parental capacity, and family stability are interconnected. Rather than focusing on blame or punishment, familist CPS advocacy strengthens the entire family system—supporting parents, caregivers, and children together—while aligning with CPS expectations and court requirements.
